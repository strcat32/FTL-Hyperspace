# FTL: Duels module

This module turns Hyperspace into the base of **FTL: Duels**, a 1v1 real-time PvP mode for FTL: Faster Than Light. It lives in this folder. Outside it there are a few small changes: one line in `CMakeLists.txt` links Winsock (`ws2_32`) for the netcode, and two functions added to Hyperspace's function list for Windows (`libzhlgen/test/functions/win32/1.6.9/`): `graphics_read_pixels` in `Global.zhl` (screenshots) and `BatterySystem::SetTurnedOn` in `BatterySystem.zhl` (the backup battery's button). In `src/game/UserInterface/CustomHotkeys.cpp`, the console key defaults to Tab (many keyboards have no backslash key), and the chat key ("duels_chat", B) takes the place of Hyperspace's speed toggle. `src/game/Gameplay/World/CrewAbilities.cpp` makes two calls into this module (crew powers in a duel, below).

## Current state: the match (phase 3, 0.8.0-dev)

- **Two players over UDP:** one hosts, the other joins, directly or through a relay server. Each game owns its own ship; the opponent is ship 1, a replica built from the opponent's loadout and driven by their messages (split authority).
- **State sync:** 10 times a second, hull, shields, system power, damage and bonus power (Zoltan crew: the replica takes its owner's, not what its puppets in a room would give), weapon power and charge, artillery charge, the lock on each system (ion damage, and the backup battery and the cloak while they run and cool down) with its timer, the battery, and the cloak (on, and how far its time has run). The replica's cloak goes on and off only with its owner's (`CloakingSystem::SetTurnedOn` refuses everything else for it): while it runs, this game's weapons stop charging, as against any cloaked ship. The owner's game decides the rest (its length, a shot fired taking a fifth off it unless the ship has Stealth Weapons, the evasion). The replica's power is set the way the owner's changed: taken away as ion and damage take it, added as the power bars add it; subsystems (a nebula switches the sensors off) take the owner's value and keep it, whatever this game's environment does.
- **Shots decided by the defender:** a shot is sent with its exact target point when it leaves the weapon. The defender's own game decides dodge, shields and damage, and sends the verdict back. The attacker's copy waits at the target's shield until the verdict arrives, then plays it out. Lasers (burst and ion too), missiles and flak shards work this way. A bomb's copy waits where it goes off, then goes off or misses as the defender's did. A beam's copy sweeps the replica without damage; the defender's game does the damage. Artillery (the Federation cruisers) fires as FTL has it, by itself at a room FTL picks, and its shots go like a weapon's (from its system, by index); the replica's artillery charges with its owner's but never fires on its own (`ProjectileFactory::ReadyToFire` says no during its loop). A crystal shard (Crystal Vengeance) goes to the defender when it crosses into their space (FTL loses some that break off away from the enemy), from where it broke off.
- **Drones:** each game flies its own drones; the opponent's drones on our screen are puppets that are launched, powered, moved and destroyed as their owner's are, and never act on their own. What happens in a space is decided by the game whose space it is: our defense drones shoot the opponent's shots down in our space (the verdict "downed", shown on both screens), and hits on the opponent's drones in our space are reported to their owner. A combat drone's shot travels like a weapon's shot and is decided by the defender. Combat, beam, defense, anti-combat, hull repair and shield drones work this way; boarding drones and the drones that work inside ships are below (Boarding). The puppets are drawn behind their owner's updates by the measured latency plus one update interval, so they move smoothly at lag.
- **Timing:** the defender's copy flies out of the enemy window faster, to make up for the network delay. It enters the defender's space when the attacker's copy entered the enemy window on the attacker's screen.
- **Duel view:** our ship stays as FTL draws it. The enemy window grows to the left up to a gap after it, and moves below the jump and menu buttons when it reaches them. The opponent's ship is drawn in it as large as it fits (about 0.65 for a Kestrel), mirrored so it faces us, with its icons the right way round, on the same centre line as ours. It is drawn with smooth filtering. In windows larger than 1280 x 720, frames are drawn at the window's size, so it keeps its detail. Mouse input goes through the inverse transforms: aiming at rooms, beam lines, crew orders, doors and tooltips work as usual. (`view equal on` draws both ships at one scale instead.)
- **Crew:** the opponent's crew in our game are puppets of theirs: the owner's roster (ids, species, names, skills) replaces the replica's default crew, and the state carries where each one is and is heading, their health, and whether they are dead. Puppets walk there with FTL's own pathfinding (and are put in place if they drift more than a tile and a half for a second); nothing changes their health or lets them repair the replica. They are shown repairing, fighting fires, fighting and typing at consoles as their owners are (the flags FTL gives its crew animation). A cloned crew member is a new one in FTL and comes back as a new roster entry (tested with a clone bay). Health goes as whole points, at least 1 while alive (FTL's crew live on at 0.4).
- **Rooms:** oxygen per room, burning tiles, hull breaches, doors and airlocks, and crystal lockdowns come with the state. The replica's own environment (fire spreading, air leaking) doesn't run; the owner's values are held every frame, so puppets fighting fires or patching breaches change nothing there.
- **Hacking** (`DuelsHacking.*`, design: `docs/design/hacking.md` in the FTL: Duels repository): in the defender's game, the replica's hacking system does the hacking, with FTL's own code. The attacker's game says which system its drone went for; the replica's drone flies there in the defender's space (its defense drones may shoot it down), and the defender says whether it attached; the attacker's pulse waits for that. The pulse (the owner's lock) gives the defender's system FTL's hacked effect for as long, and the replica's systems show their owner's hacking. A drone at W1 or D1 hacks bay 1. Hacking drones join the space's drone list when the ships meet (Hyperspace adds them only with an enemy present).
- **Mind control** (`DuelsMind.*`): the attacker's mind control picks the crew; the defender's game puts those crew (by roster id) under the replica's mind control, whose timer follows the owner's, and releases them with it. The puppets show their owner's mind control, and the attacker's orders to them go to the defender's game (`ShipManager::CommandCrewMoveRoom`).
- **Boarding** (`DuelsBoarding.*`, design: `docs/design/boarding.md` in the FTL: Duels repository): crew aboard the opponent's ship are simulated by the game whose ship it is. The attacker's teleporter sends its crew as usual; when they arrive, the defender's game makes them as intruders on its own ship (guests, under their owner's ids), and the attacker sees them as puppets driven by the guest entries of the defender's crew state. The attacker's orders to them go to the defender's game, and FTL's crew AI moves neither guests nor puppets. When the attacker's teleporter takes them back (Hyperspace's `CompleteShip::InitiateTeleport`), the defender's game reports each one's health and skills (or death) and moves it onto its copy of the attacker's ship, a puppet again; the attacker's game gives it that health at home. The replica's systems take damage only from their owner's state, and the replica never teleports on its own (FTL's enemy ship takes its hurt boarders back by itself). A boarding drone's pod flies in the defender's space as the defender's copy of it (`MSG_POD`): the defender's defense drones may shoot it down, FTL picks where it lands there, and the defender tells the attacker where it goes, where it landed or that it was shot down (`MSG_POD_RESULT`); the attacker's pod flies to that point and lands only after that word, and its robot is the puppet of the defender's guest entry. Crew drones (anti-personnel, system repair) are in their owner's roster with their drone slot; the replica's own drone in that slot is their puppet. Puppets walk to where a still owner stands rather than ahead to its goal. Crew powers (Hyperspace's; in vanilla the crystal lockdown) are their owner's to use: ours used aboard the replica go to the defender (`MSG_CREW_POWER`), whose game uses the power for its guest; the opponent's crew never use theirs by themselves in our game (Hyperspace lets enemy crew do that in a fight).
- **Augments** travel in the loadout: the replica has its owner's, so those that act on the other ship work in the game where they act (the Defense Scrambler on our defense drones, Hacking Stun with the replica's hacking; Zoltan Shield Bypass on bombs travels with the shot).
- **Weapon and drone bays** (`DuelsBays.*`, design: `docs/design/weapon-bays.md` in the FTL: Duels repository): as FTL loads a player ship's layout, its weapons room and drone control room are cut into one 1x1 room per weapon and drone slot, each with a subsystem `weapon_bay_1` .. `_4` or `drone_bay_1` .. `_3` (registered in the FTL: Duels data mod) with as many bars as its weapon or drone needs. A weapon or drone is off while its bay is damaged, ioned or hacked (its box turns red); the weapons system (in W1, with the gunner console) and drone control take no damage directly. Their power bar is drawn per weapon (drone) in slot order, so a bay's damage and repair show red in the middle of the bar where its weapon's bars are (blue when ioned, purple when hacked), with the manning and other icons once above the whole bar. Their bars beyond what the weapons need are buffer points: damage on a bay breaks those spare bars first, and the crew repairs them in W1 (D1) after bay 1 (`CrewAI::SelectRepair`). Our own bays have no box in the subsystem panel; the enemy window places the opponent's system icons in as many rows as fit. The bays look like one room (no walls or doors between them: those doors leave the ship's door list and stay open) and their icons show the kind of weapon or drone, redrawn when they move; an empty slot shows nothing. A player who drags weapons or drones to other slots sends the loadout again. `DroneBox::StatusColor` was added to Hyperspace's function list for Windows (`libzhlgen/test/functions/win32/1.6.9/DroneBox.zhl`). Crew experience: in a duel each skill gain counts as often as the host's `xp` setting says (×3 by default).
- **Relay:** players who can't reach each other directly (routers) connect through a relay server (`server/relay` in the FTL: Duels repository, Rust). The host opens a room and gets a six-character code; the other player joins with it. After a cookie handshake, every packet is signed with a key per player, and the relay forwards the duel's packets without reading them. Protocol: `docs/design/relay-protocol.md` in the FTL: Duels repository.
- **The match** (`DuelsRounds.*`, `DuelsRefit.*`, `DuelsWindow.*`; design: `docs/design/match-flow.md` in the FTL: Duels repository): best of N rounds, each a timed preparation (a full repair, the dead captain back, the round's scrap, a shop, upgrades) and a fight. The host's game runs it and sends the match state; each game reports its own defeat and the damage its ship took (the damage score). Forfeit, conceding a round, draw offers for the round or the match, and an anti-stall timer. A lost connection doesn't end the match: the player cut off has 60 s to come back. FTL's own endings (game over, a derelict enemy) are off in a match. A round's fight can be near a sun, a pulsar or in an asteroid field (`DuelsEnvironment.*`, design: `docs/design/environment.md`): the same schedule of flares, pulses or rocks in both games, from the host's seed, each game's hazards acting on its own ship. The DUELS button next to the options button opens the Duels window: the match, how to win, the bay icons, and the buttons Ready, Concede round, the draw offers and Forfeit.
- **Console, feed and chat:** the console key (Options → Controls; Tab by default, so F1 selects crew member 1 as in FTL) opens the console at the top left: the log's last 12 lines on a dark backdrop and an input line with a caret. It stays open after a command until the key or Escape; Up and Down bring back earlier lines. `DUEL` can be left out, and `HS <command>` runs Hyperspace's own commands (in debug mode). Letters come out as typed. The log shows only there (debug mode also shows each new line at the top left for a moment). Short status lines (a player joins, a round starts, its result) and the chat go to the feed at the bottom left, above the power bars; the chat key (B by default) opens the chat with more lines and a "Say:" line. Chat lines are held to 120 bytes and 5 lines per 10 s, by the sender and again by the receiver (a flooding opponent is muted for 30 s). The network numbers (`netstats on`) show at the top right, under the "FTL: Duels" label; the window title says "FTL: Duels" too.
- **Missing images:** FTL draws an image it can't find as its warning sign (`img/nullResource.png`); each missing name goes to `duels_log.txt` once ("Resources: image ... is missing").
- **Debug mode:** commands that change ships or automate play (refits, damage, AI, scripts, `keys`, Hyperspace's commands, ...) work only in debug mode: `debug on`, `DUELS_DEBUG=1`, or a test scenario. It stays on until the game restarts, and the other player of every duel after it is told ("DEBUG DUEL").
- **Connection:** round trips come from the game's own packets; the link sends a heartbeat only after 1 s without traffic, and the relay gets a PING only after 3 s without a packet from it. A peer silent for 10 s is gone. Through a relay, the relay decides who is still there: the player who still reaches it wins the fight, the other one is told the connection is lost. A game that leaves or closes says so at once. `netstats on` shows the round trip, losses and traffic in three lines at the bottom left.
- **From step 1:** enemy AI replaced by commands, no pause, speed locked to normal, tracing, and the autotest harness that plays a scripted match without a human.

Results and findings: `docs/dev/step2-results.md` in the FTL: Duels repository.

## Command language

The same commands are used by scenario scripts, the in-game console (F1, then `<verb> …`, with or without `DUEL`) and the autotest harness. Ship ids are 0 (the local player) and 1 (the opponent).

| Group | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Network duel | `debug [on]`, `netstats on\|off`, `host [port] [local]`, `join <address> [port]`, `relay [server[:port]]`, `host relay [server[:port]] [name <room name>] [password <password>] [unlisted]`, `join relay <code\|@file> [server[:port]] [password <password>]`, `join <code> [password]` (with a relay set), `lobby [page]` (the relay's open rooms), `leave`, `net` (status), `name <player>`, `say <text>` (chat), `xp [factor]` (crew experience, the host's counts), `netsim <delay ms> [jitter ms] [loss %]`, `netsim cut on\|off` (a pulled cable: nothing goes out or comes in), `chatflood <count>` (test: chat lines past the sender's limits) |
| Match (3.1) | `match` (status), `match rounds [<n>]\|prep <seconds>\|stall <seconds>\|permadeath on\|off\|env auto\|off\|sun\|pulsar\|asteroids\|free` (the host's settings for the next duel; `stall`: the anti-stall timer, 0 for off; `env`: the fights' environments; `free`: one fight without rounds, as before), `ready` (the preparation ends when both are), `forfeit`, `concede` (this round), `draw [round\|match]` (offer), `draw yes\|no` (answer), `duels [open\|close]`, `duels click <x> <y>` (the Duels window, for tests) |
| Session | `version`, `status`, `nopause on\|off`, `trace on\|off`, `tracepower on\|off`, `ai <ship> on\|off`, `script <file>`, `stop`, `note <text>`, `quit` |
| Power and weapons | `power <ship> <system> <level>`, `weapon <ship> <slot> on\|off`, `fire <ship> <slot> room <room>`, `autofire <ship> <slot> on\|off` |
| Crew and systems | `crew <ship> <index> room <room>`, `crewpower <index> [power]` (our crew member uses a crew power), `door <ship> <id>\|all open\|close` (all: every door and airlock), `cloak <ship>`, `augment <ship> <AUGMENT> [off]`, `hack <system>\|room <room>\|pulse\|stop`, `mind room <room>\|order <room>`, `teleport send\|recall\|order <room>` |
| View and console | `view [auto\|fit\|off]` (the duel view: for the duel opponent, for any enemy, or vanilla), `view equal\|hires\|icons on\|off` (both ships at one scale; window-size frames; icons turned the right way round), `aimcheck [full]` (the mouse over every room of both ships, through the game's own mouse handling), `console [text]`, `keys <text>`, `mouse <x> <y> [seconds]` (FTL's mouse held there, for tooltips), `fonttest [seconds]` (the match display's text in each of FTL's fonts) |
| Setup and diagnostics | `spawn <SHIP_BLUEPRINT>`, `arm <ship> <slot> <WEAPON_BLUEPRINT>`, `upgrade <ship> <system> <levels>`, `install <ship> <system>`, `drone <ship> <slot> <DRONE_BLUEPRINT>`, `droneparts <ship> <count>`, `dronepower <ship> <slot> on\|off`, `battery <ship> on\|off`, `ionize <ship> <system> <amount>`, `supershield <ship> <layers>`, `nebula on\|off`, `export <ship> <file>`, `describe <ship>` (with scrap, missiles, drone parts; each system's manning, each crew member's task and what they man), `hull <ship> <points>` (0 destroys the ship), `kill <ship> crew`, `rooms <ship>` (rooms, consoles and systems), `swap <slot> <slot>` (our weapons change places), `pausetest <kind>`, `window <x> <y> [width height]`, `screenshot <file.bmp>` |

- The default port is 47620 (UDP). `host local` accepts only a second game on the same computer and triggers no firewall prompt.
- `join` takes an IPv4 or IPv6 address or a host name.
- `relay` sets the relay server for `host relay` and `join relay` (UDP port 47700 by default); both also take it directly. `host relay` announces the room code, which the other player passes to `join relay` (or `@file`: the code is read from that file, for tests). Without a relay set, the first `relay` line of `duels.cfg` (in the game folder) is used; `duels.cfg` also keeps the player's name (`name` saves it; a new player starts as "Captain_" and a crew name).
- A relay room has a name, and a password or none; it shows in the relay's room list (`lobby`) unless it is `unlisted`. `join <code> [password]` joins a room at the relay without its address.
- With `trace on`, a duel writes `duels_shots.csv` (every shot, both directions) and `duels_sync.csv` (every change of our ship and of the replica).
- `screenshot` saves the next frame from FTL's own frame buffer, so it works with the window covered or the computer locked.
- `keys` types into the game through its own input handlers: characters as they are, and `{f1}`, `{tab}`, `{console}` and `{chat}` (the hotkeys as set in the options), `{enter}`, `{esc}`, `{up}`, `{down}`, `{back}` for keys (console tests).
- Scenario directive `@nodebug`: a test scenario without debug mode (`tests/scenarios/debug-check.txt`). `@ship <BLUEPRINT>`: the harness starts the game with that player ship instead of the hangar's (e.g. `PLAYER_SHIP_FED` for artillery).
- Test runs keep the games off the main monitor: with `DUELS_DISPLAY` (`x,y,width,height` of another monitor) and `DUELS_DISPLAY_SCALE`, window positions count from that monitor's corner and are scaled; `DUELS_WINDOW` (`x,y`) places the window there at start (`DuelsWin32.h`).
- `arm`, `upgrade`, `install`, `drone` and `droneparts` prepare test ships (e.g. flak, beams and bombs, a backup battery, drone control with drones); in a duel, before connecting, so the loadout carries them. `battery` and `dronepower` press the battery's and a drone's button; `ionize` does ion damage to a system, as an ion shot does (a bay by its name too, `weapon_bay_2`); `supershield` gives a ship a Zoltan super shield; `nebula` puts our ship under a nebula beacon's effect (sensors off), in this game only.

## Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `Duels.h`, `DuelsCore.cpp` | Shared state, log (`duels_log.txt`), frame trace, script runner |
| `DuelsHooks.cpp` | All hooks except the screenshot's |
| `DuelsDriver.cpp`, `DuelsShipControl.*` | The verbs, the AI takeover and projectile tracing |
| `DuelsScript.*` | The command/script parser |
| `DuelsAutotest.cpp` | The harness |
| `DuelsMatch.*` | The duel: loadout, replica, state sync, shots and verdicts |
| `DuelsRounds.*` | The match flow: rounds of preparation and fight, best of N, the score (damage taken, in the state), ready, forfeit, concede and draws; the host's game runs it (`docs/design/match-flow.md`) |
| `DuelsRefit.*` | Our ship between rounds: the captain and the crew to bring back, the round's scrap, the shop, full repair, crew home at the round's end |
| `DuelsEnvironment.*` | The fight's environment (a sun, a pulsar or an asteroid field): the host's roll, the schedule both games share, FTL's flares, pulses and rocks on our own ship only |
| `DuelsCrew.*` | Crew: ids and roster, crew in the state, the replica's crew as puppets |
| `DuelsRooms.*` | Rooms: oxygen, fires, breaches, doors and lockdowns in the state, held on the replica |
| `DuelsHacking.*` | Hacking: launches and their outcome, the replica's hacking of our systems, the pulse timer |
| `DuelsMind.*` | Mind control: the crew taken, the replica's control of ours, orders to crew under our control |
| `DuelsBoarding.*` | Boarding: crew sent aboard and taken back, their health on return, what the crew AI may do with guests and puppets, boarding drones' pods |
| `DuelsBays.*` | Weapon bays: the layouts cut as they load, the bay systems, weapons switched off with their bays, the icons by kind of weapon, the cut rooms drawn as one, the power bar per weapon, buffer points |
| `DuelsDrones.*` | Drones: their state in the sync, the replica's drones as puppets, drone hits, copies of the opponent's defense drone shots |
| `DuelsNet.*` | Session: host/join (directly or through the relay), handshake, timeouts, test conditions |
| `DuelsRelay.*`, `DuelsCrypto.*` | The relay client (cookie handshake, room codes, signed packets; no game headers, unit-tested outside the game) and SHA-256/HMAC for it |
| `DuelsConfig.*` | `duels.cfg`: the player's name and the relay servers |
| `DuelsWindow.*` | The DUELS button and the Duels window: the match, how to win, the bay icons, Ready, Concede round, the draw offers, Forfeit |
| `DuelsConsole.*` | The console (input line, the log's recent lines, command history), the feed and the chat at the bottom left, the console and chat keys |
| `DuelsHud.*` | What FTL: Duels draws over the interface: the network numbers at the top right, the font test |
| `DuelsLink.*`, `DuelsWire.h` | Reliable-UDP link and message encoding (no game headers; unit-tested outside the game) |
| `DuelsSocket.*` | UDP sockets (Windows, and POSIX for later) |
| `DuelsView.*` | The duel view: layout, the enemy window's size and frame, both ships' transforms, mouse mapping |
| `DuelsScreen.*` | Frames at the window's size, screenshots from the frame buffer, the "FTL: Duels" label and window title |
| `DuelsTrace.*`, `DuelsWin32.*` | CSV output, window helpers (minimize, move, the test display) |
